## :page_facing_up: **Abstract:**  
Basalt formations emerge as promising Carbon Dioxide (CO2) sinks facilitating rapid mineralization that transforms CO2 into a solid state and mitigates leakage risks. However, mineralization processes at an industrial scale present significant uncertainty, and traditional numerical simulations are time-consuming and resource-intensive. To address this, we propose a surrogate model to accelerate the prediction of carbonate precipitation by integrating deep learning proxies. Our model incorporates seven initial minerals, four precipitation minerals, and the critical factor of porosity changes. It achieves computational efficiency that is 20,000 times faster than traditional numerical models while maintaining high accuracy. Using a combined approach of surrogate and numerical models, we conducted a parameter analysis across multiple injection scenarios. We found that the porosity of the reservoir near the injection well undergoes a self-enhancing to self-sealing process, which leads to a decline in injectivity during the later stages of injection. However, placing the injection well at the top of the reservoir can mitigate this issue, reducing the decline in injectivity and minimizing pressure buildup. From the perspective of storage efficiency, positioning the injection well at the bottom of the reservoir and lowering the injection rate enhances the overall efficiency of CO2 sequestration. Notably, magnesite exhibited the highest accumulation around the injection well, potentially having the most significant long-term impact on reservoir stability. This advancement highlights the promise of deep learning techniques in accelerating and enhancing the assessment of CO2 mineralization processes in Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS) projects, offering a computationally feasible solution for forecasting complex geochemical interactions.
